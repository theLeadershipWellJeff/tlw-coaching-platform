# theLeadershipWell — Writing Standards

**Version 1.0** · *Living document*
**Owner:** Dr. Jeff K. Holmes
**Purpose:** Define how theLeadershipWell writes — across every channel, in every register, by every person or system producing copy on its behalf.

Three audiences, three entry points:

| If you are… | Start here |
|---|---|

## 1. The Non-Negotiables

Five rules. They apply to every channel, without exception. Everything else in this document is craft guidance; these are policy.

1. **No unverified statistics. Ever.** If a number can't be traced to a primary source you have actually seen, it does not ship. See §4.2 for the permanent exclusion list.
2. **Attribution is precise.** Naming the wrong originator of a framework is a credibility event, not a footnote. See §4.3.
3. **One CTA per piece.** Two asks is zero asks.
4. **No jargon dressed as insight.** If a Korn Ferry partner could have written the sentence, rewrite it.
5. **Nothing moralizes.** We are tenaciously curious and not judgmental. That's a value, and it shows up sentence by sentence.

## 2. The Core Voice

### 2.1 In One Sentence

> *Confident and warm. Research-grounded. Never preachy. Never salesy.*

### 2.2 The Six Principles

**Direct, with warmth.** Say the thing. Say it like a colleague who respects the reader's time, not a consultant performing expertise.

**Evidence-led — and honest about the edges.** When there's research, cite it. When there isn't, say so plainly. "I don't have research on this, but here's what I see in the coaching room" is a stronger sentence than a vague appeal to studies.

**Curious, not judgmental.** We describe what we observe. We don't scold the reader for having the problem.

**Plain language.** Senior leaders are smart and busy. Complexity in the thinking, simplicity in the sentence.

**Specific over general.** "Three CEOs in financial services this quarter" beats "many of our clients." "The chair where I coach, code, write, and put out fires" beats "my usual workspace."

**Vulnerable with authority.** Jeff is simultaneously the expert and the person still figuring it out. Both are true, both stay in the writing. The vulnerability is what makes the authority tolerable.

### 2.3 Signature Moves

These are Jeff's fingerprints. Use them; don't overuse them. Two or three per piece, not seven.

| Move | Example | Where it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Conversational opener** | "It struck me this week that confidence has a lot of layers." / "Hi! It has been a bit since we have connected." | LinkedIn, newsletter, 1:1 email |
| **The "So, um, yeah —" close** | Signature sign-off, followed by a reflective question. Refuses to land the plane neatly. | LinkedIn only. Never in proposals, app copy, or client-facing formal work. |
| **NOT/BUT contrast** | "That's important, but it's missing the bigger opportunity sitting right in front of them." | Everywhere. This is the workhorse. |
| **Reflective closing question** | "What are you managing right now that might be a symptom?" | LinkedIn, newsletter, workshop |
| **Coaching-room story → research → tool** | The book's chapter rhythm. Story first, always. | Book, newsletter, workshop, long-form |
| **Named framework, delivered plainly** | BART, HERO, The Climb, PSI, Colors of Change, SPARK | Everywhere — this is the IP |
| **Reader-as-colleague direct address** | "Think of the last meeting you had to attend." | LinkedIn, newsletter, email |
| **Honest self-implication** | "I had gotten in the practice of doing my important work from the same chair…" / "I have been accused of being too flexible." | Anywhere the reader might otherwise feel lectured |

### 2.4 The Two-Question Voice Check

Before anything publishes:

1. **Would a CEO at a $10M company forward this to her CFO without embarrassment?**
2. **Could a Korn Ferry partner have written this?**

Yes to (1), no to (2) = on brand.

## 3. The Anti-Pattern Register

### 3.1 Never Say

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Hype** | "Transform your leadership," "game-changing," "10x your," "secret weapon" |
| **Guru framing** | "What nobody's telling you," "the truth about," "most leaders will never understand" |
| **Generic coach-speak** | "Unlock your potential," "lean into the discomfort," "show up authentically," "hold space" |
| **Corporate jargon** | "Synergies," "leveraging," "circling back," "double-click on," "at the end of the day" |
| **Performative humility** | "I'm just a coach who…," "I'm no expert, but…" |
| **False urgency** | "Only 3 spots left," countdown pressure, artificial scarcity |
| **Engagement bait** | "Agree?" "Thoughts?" "Comment YES below," "Save this for later" |

### 3.2 AI-Detection Tells — The Newer Problem

Readers are getting fast at spotting machine-written copy, and the penalty is severe: the moment a reader suspects AI, everything else in the piece loses credibility. This matters disproportionately for theLeadershipWell because the entire value proposition is *a real person with real judgment*.

**The tells, ranked by how badly they give the game away:**

| Tell | What it looks like | Why it reads as machine |
|---|---|---|
| **Staccato uniformity** | Every paragraph one line. Each landing a little harder than the last. Like this. | Originated on LinkedIn, models trained on it, now it's the house style of AI-drafted posts |
| **Negation-correction** | "Not the wrong time. The wrong place." "It's not about X. It's about Y." | Single most reliable fingerprint currently in circulation |
| **Tricolon habit** | Three-beat lists everywhere. "Different energy. Different focus. Different results." | Humans use two beats or five, unevenly |
| **Em-dash per paragraph** | Consistent rhythmic dash placement in every graf | Distribution is too regular to be human |
| **Over-tidy resolution** | Setup and payoff match perfectly; nothing is left hanging | Real thinking leaves loose threads |
| **Absence of load-bearing junk** | Every detail serves the argument; nothing is just *true* | Humans include specifics that don't earn their keep |
| **No self-correction** | The piece never doubles back, hedges, or says "well, sort of" | Nobody thinks in a straight line |
| **"Here's the thing" / "Here's what I've learned"** | Transitional throat-clearing that signals a reveal | Overused to the point of parody |

**The counter-moves — write these in deliberately:**

- **Uneven paragraph weight.** One paragraph that runs long because the thought ran long, sitting next to a three-word one.
- **Detail that doesn't serve the argument.** "The chair where I coach, code, write, chase business development, and put out fires" — that list is longer than it needs to be, and that's exactly why it reads as real. Don't trim it.
- **Mid-piece self-correction.** A "well, sort of" or a parenthetical that walks something back. Nearly impossible to fake, instantly disarming.
- **Endings that don't fully resolve.** The "So, um, yeah —" close works precisely because it refuses to conclude.
- **Occasional imperfect syntax.** A sentence that runs on. A fragment. Not stylized — actually a little loose.

### 3.3 The AI Tell Test

Read the draft aloud. If it sounds like it's being *performed* rather than *said*, it fails. Then check: are there at least two moments in the piece where the writing does something inefficient?

If no — it's too clean. Loosen it.

### 3.4 A Standing Rule on Editing

When an AI assistant or editor tightens Jeff's copy, **the tightening must be flagged**, not silently applied. Jeff decides whether the looseness was doing work. Bulk is often a feature.

## 4. Evidence & Citation Standards

Credibility protection is the highest-value discipline in this business. It is also the easiest thing to lose in a single careless post.

### 4.1 Evidence Labeling

Every substantive claim in drafted content carries one of three labels in the working document (not in the published piece):

- **`[RESEARCH-BACKED]`** — traceable to a peer-reviewed source, meta-analysis, or primary dataset. Citation included.
- **`[PRACTITIONER]`** — sourced to credible practitioner analysis (HBR, MIT SMR, McKinsey Quarterly) that is not itself peer-reviewed. Named as such.
- **`[OPINION]` / `[EXPERIENCE]`** — Jeff's judgment or n=1 observation. Framed in the published piece as personal, never as finding.

Every content deliverable ships with an **evidence ledger** — a table mapping each claim to its label and source, plus an explicit list of anything intentionally omitted for lack of verification.

### 4.2 Permanent Exclusion List

These figures circulate widely, are frequently attributed to credible institutions, and do not survive contact with a primary source. **They never appear in any theLeadershipWell content, in any channel, for any reason.**

| Excluded claim | Commonly attributed to | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "CEOs make ~35,000 decisions daily" | UNC research | No traceable primary source |
| "73% of CEOs report high stress levels" | Harvard Business Review | No traceable primary source |
| "Images get 2.3x engagement on LinkedIn" | Various marketing blogs | Unverified; circulates without primary source. Do not cite. |

**Adding to this list:** any statistic that survives two search attempts without producing a primary source goes on the list. Removing from the list requires the actual primary source in hand.

The underlying *themes* — decision fatigue, executive stress, visual content performance — remain valid and usable. They just need better support than these three numbers.

### 4.3 Attribution Precision Register

Getting the originator right is a differentiator. Most content in this space gets these wrong.

| Framework | Correct attribution | Common error |
|---|---|---|
| **Time Management Matrix (four quadrants)** | Covey, *The 7 Habits* (1989) built the matrix. Eisenhower *named the tension* in a 1954 address, quoting an unnamed former college president. | Crediting the matrix to Eisenhower |
| **"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"** | Marilyn Strathern (1997) coined this phrasing. Goodhart's original (1975) was narrower and about monetary policy. | Attributing the popular phrasing to Goodhart |
| **Positive Intelligence / Saboteurs** | Shirzad Chamine — label as a *coaching framework*, not peer-reviewed research | Presenting as research-validated |
| **PsyCap / HERO** | Luthans, Avolio, Avey, Youssef — extensive peer-reviewed base | Generally cited correctly; keep it that way |
| **Adaptive leadership** | Heifetz (and Linsky, Grashow) | Fine as-is |
| **Hope Theory** | C.R. Snyder | Often conflated with generic optimism — distinguish |

### 4.4 Approved Citation Library

Sources verified and in regular rotation. Cite freely.

- Avey, Reichard, Luthans & Mhatre (2011) — PsyCap meta-analysis, 51 samples, ~12,600 participants, *Human Resource Development Quarterly*
- Bandura (1997) — *Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control*
- Stajkovic & Luthans (1998) — self-efficacy and work performance meta-analysis
- Judge & Bono (2001) — core self-evaluations and job satisfaction/performance
- Luthans, Avolio, Avey & Norman (2007) — PsyCap measurement, *Personnel Psychology*
- Holmes, J. K. (2023) — PsyCap during organizational change (HEC Paris & Oxford)
- Snyder — Hope Theory
- Kaplan (1995) — Attention Restoration Theory, *Journal of Environmental Psychology*
- Godden & Baddeley (1975) — context-dependent memory
- Rogers — *Diffusion of Innovations*
- Gallup — *State of the Global Workplace* (annual; cite the specific year's report)

### 4.5 The Honesty Clause

When we don't know, we say we don't know. This is a brand asset, not a weakness. "I don't have a verified figure for that, and I'm not going to hand you an unverified one" is on-brand and builds more trust than a confident number would.

## 5. Channel Standards

The core voice holds everywhere. What shifts is **register, length, structure, and CTA behavior.**

---

### 5.1 LinkedIn Posts

**Purpose:** Top of funnel. Build category authority, generate inbound conversations, feed the sales pipeline.
**Cadence:** Twice weekly — Tuesday and Thursday. Occasional Friday/weekend.
**Length:** 120–220 words is the target band. Hard ceiling 3,000 characters.
**Register:** Most personal of any channel. Lead with a specific moment.

**Structure:**
1. **Hook (lines 1–2).** Everything before the "see more" fold. Must carry tension or curiosity. A one-word question is not a hook.
2. **The moment.** A specific, dated, concrete scene from the coaching room, a client conversation, or Jeff's own life.
3. **The turn.** What the moment revealed. Where the framework or research enters.
4. **The gift.** Something the reader can do Monday morning.
5. **The close.** "So, um, yeah —" plus a reflective question. Not a CTA.

**Rules:**
- **No links in the post body.** External links suppress organic reach. CTA link goes in the **first comment**, posted within 60 seconds of publishing.
- **One CTA maximum.**
- **Hashtags: 0–3.** Ranked by expected reach. See §5.3 for the honest caveat on hashtag value.
- Every post addresses at least one named CEO challenge (see §5.14).
- Bold/italic markers do not render on LinkedIn — convert to unicode or remove.
- Post-mortem to the scoring log at 48 hours: impressions, comments, profile views, booked calls attributed, and **edit weight** (light / moderate / near-total rewrite).

**Imagery:** Authentic photography outperforms designed cards on personal-narrative posts. AI-generated imagery underperforms on non-AI topics — documented in the scoring log, avoid it. Designed cards are appropriate for framework posts. See Brand Guidelines §4 for Signal Orange discipline.

**Sequential architecture:** Thursday posts may explicitly reference Tuesday's to drive return traffic. When used, the posts become order-dependent — don't publish out of sequence.

---

### 5.2 LinkedIn Comments & DMs

**Purpose:** Where the reach actually comes from, and where leads self-identify.
**Length:** 1–4 sentences for comments. 3–5 for DMs.
**Register:** Warmer and looser than posts. Closer to speech.

**Rules:**
- Reply to every substantive comment within the first two hours — comment velocity drives distribution.
- **Never pitch in a comment reply.** Answer the question, add one thing they didn't ask for, stop.
- Commenters who name a specific struggle have self-identified. That's a warm list — route to outreach, not to a pitch in the thread.
- DMs never open with the ask. Reference the specific thing they said. One question. No link in the first message.
- No "Thanks for sharing!" No emoji-only replies.

---

### 5.3 Other Social Channels

**Applies to:** X, Instagram, YouTube descriptions, Threads.

**Standing position:** LinkedIn is the primary channel and gets the investment. Other platforms carry repurposed material, not original production, until there's evidence to justify the effort.

| Platform | Register shift | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **X** | Tighter, more argumentative. The reflective close doesn't work here. | Single-insight extracts from LinkedIn posts. Threads only for framework content. |
| **Instagram** | Visual-first, caption secondary. Framework cards and quote cards. | Brand Guidelines govern entirely. Caption is 1–3 sentences plus the reflective question. |
| **YouTube / video descriptions** | Functional and scannable. Voice is minimal. | First two lines carry the value proposition. Timestamps. One link. |

**On hashtags generally:** LinkedIn's hashtag-driven distribution has weakened substantially. Hashtags now function mainly as topical classification for the algorithm rather than as a discovery channel. Dwell time and early comment velocity matter far more. Cap at three and don't over-invest.

---

### 5.4 Newsletter

**Purpose:** Depth. The place where a LinkedIn post's idea gets its full argument. Nurture, not acquisition.
**Length:** 700–1,200 words.
**Register:** Editorial. This is the closest channel to the book's voice.
**Structure:** Roughly eight sections, with one core editorial distinction as the spine.

**Rules:**
- **One core idea per issue.** Resist the roundup format.
- Longer paragraphs are permitted and encouraged here — this is not social.
- Cite sources inline, with links.
- The "So, um, yeah —" close is optional here and often too casual. A closing question is still right.
- **One CTA**, at the end. Links are permitted in-body (unlike LinkedIn).
- Subject line: specific and unsalesy. Curiosity without clickbait. Test against "would this embarrass me in a CEO's inbox?"
- Preview text is a second headline, not a repeat of the subject.

**LinkedIn → newsletter fold:** Two related LinkedIn posts typically stack into one newsletter with two or three new sections bridging them. This is the default production path, not a shortcut.

**HubSpot constraint:** Custom HTML email modules accept **body-content snippets only**. No `<!DOCTYPE>`, `<html>`, `<head>`, or `<body>` wrapper tags. No `<style>` blocks — all styling inline.

---

### 5.5 Marketing Email (Nurture, Lead Magnet, Re-engagement)

**Purpose:** Move a known contact one step along the funnel.
**Length:** 150–300 words. Shorter than the newsletter, always.
**Register:** Warm, direct, and unmistakably from a person. Written as if to one reader.

**Rules:**
- **Write to one person, not a list.** "You" singular. Never "many of you."
- Open with the reason for the email, not with pleasantries.
- **One CTA.** One link. Repeated at most twice (mid and end) for longer emails.
- No banner-heavy design. Brand Guidelines apply, but restraint reads as personal; polish reads as marketing.
- Sign as Jeff, not as theLeadershipWell.
- Sequences: state the exit condition and the branching logic in the working doc. Nobody should receive email 4 after booking.

| Email type | Opening move | CTA |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead magnet delivery** | Deliver the thing immediately, above the fold | Soft — one relevant next resource |
| **Nurture** | The specific idea, no preamble | Usually none, or a soft reply-invitation |
| **Re-engagement** | Name the gap honestly ("It's been a while") | Single, low-friction — 15-min connect |
| **Launch / offer** | The problem it solves, then the offer | Direct — the booking link |

---

### 5.6 Sales & Prospecting Email

**Purpose:** Book the call. That's it.
**Length:** Under 125 words. Ruthlessly.
**Register:** Peer-to-peer. A CEO writing to a CEO.

**Structure:**
1. A specific, evidenced reason for reaching out (something they said, published, announced, or are visibly navigating)
2. One sentence of relevance — what you do, tied to their situation
3. One question or one low-friction ask

**Rules:**
- **Never lead with credentials.** The Oxford doctorate is a proof point, not an opener.
- No "I hope this finds you well." No "I wanted to reach out."
- No attachments in a first-touch email.
- The ask is a 15-minute connection, not a "quick chat about your leadership development needs."
- If you can't name a specific reason for contacting this person, don't send it.

---

### 5.7 Client Email (1:1, Active Engagement)

**Purpose:** Continuity of the coaching relationship between sessions.
**Length:** Whatever the content requires. Usually short.
**Register:** Warm but tight. The most familiar register in the system.

**Rules:**
- **Reference prior conversations specifically.** "The thing you said about your COO" beats "our last session."
- Bulleted where there are actions; prose where there's reflection.
- Prep sheets go out at least 24 hours before a session (this is a Signal Orange trigger in the workspace app — see Brand Guidelines §4.1).
- Never moralize about a missed commitment. Ask about it.
- Confidentiality is absolute. Nothing from a client engagement appears in content without explicit permission and, by default, de-identification.

**On using client material in content:** de-identify by default ("a CEO in financial services"). Ask permission for anything more specific than that. Never use a story a client would recognize as their own without asking, even if others wouldn't.

---

### 5.8 Proposals & Statements of Work

**Purpose:** Close. Also, be the artifact that makes a boutique firm look like an institution.
**Length:** As short as the scope allows. Most proposals are too long.
**Register:** Confident, calm, specific. Zero warmth-performance.

**Structure:**
1. What we heard (their words, reflected back accurately)
2. What we recommend and why
3. Scope, deliverables, timeline
4. Investment
5. What success looks like — measurable where possible

**Rules:**
- **No "So, um, yeah —."** No conversational openers. This is the one place the personal voice steps back.
- Lead with their situation, not our methodology.
- Name the frameworks; don't teach them. The proposal is not a workshop.
- Every claim about outcomes is either cited or framed as expectation, never as guarantee.
- Price is stated plainly, once, without hedging or apology.

---

### 5.9 Website & Landing Pages

**Purpose:** Convert an interested stranger into a booked call.
**Register:** Confident, calm, evidence-led. **Avoids hero-statement bravado.**

**Rules:**
- The positioning sentence is the anchor: *Research-backed executive coaching that scales your business by scaling your people.*
- Headlines describe, they don't exclaim.
- Social proof is specific and attributed. Named testimonials with role and organization beat anonymous praise.
- One primary CTA per page. Secondary CTAs are permitted only below the fold.
- No pop-ups that interrupt reading.
- Body copy in second person, present tense.

---

### 5.10 Workshop, Slide, & Facilitation Copy

**Purpose:** Support the room. Never replace the facilitator.
**Register:** Sparse on the slide, dense in the notes.

**Rules:**
- **Slides are sparse on text, dense on talking points.** If the deck reads on its own, it's a document, not a deck.
- One idea per slide. One framework per section.
- Frameworks appear as visuals, not bullet lists.
- Speaker notes carry the story, the research citation, and the transition.
- Handouts and worksheets are a separate artifact from the deck — build them as such.
- Brand Guidelines govern entirely: Shoal Bold headlines, Open Sans body, Signal Orange once per composition.

---

### 5.11 Product & App Copy (Coaching App, Workspace UI)

**Purpose:** Get the user to the next right action without friction. The interface should feel like the coach is present but not talking.

**Register shift — this is the biggest one in the system.** App copy is the *least* personal voice theLeadershipWell uses. The user is trying to do something. Personality that delays them is a cost, not a charm.

**Rules:**
- **Verbs, not nouns.** "Start a session," not "Session initiation."
- **Second person, present tense, active voice.** Always.
- **Buttons say what happens.** "Send prep sheet," not "Submit" or "Continue."
- **Under 8 words** for labels, buttons, and menu items.
- **Empty states do three things:** say what goes here, say why it's useful, offer the one action. Three sentences maximum.
- **Error messages never blame the user.** State what happened, state the fix. No "Oops!" No exclamation marks.
- **Confirmation copy names the consequence.** "Delete this session? Your notes will be removed permanently."
- **No coaching-speak in the interface.** The app is not the coach. It's the room the coaching happens in.
- **Signal Orange in the app is Tier 1 and Tier 2 only** — never Tier 3 editorial. See Brand Guidelines §4.

**Where the voice does surface:** onboarding, milestone moments, and reflection prompts. These can carry warmth and a reflective question. Everything else stays out of the way.

| Element | Bad | Good |
|---|---|---|
| Button | "Let's Go!" | "Start session" |
| Empty state | "Nothing here yet — time to unlock your potential!" | "Your prep sheets will appear here. Adding one before a session helps you use the hour better. Create your first." |
| Error | "Oops! Something went wrong." | "That didn't save. Check your connection and try again." |
| Confirmation | "Are you sure?" | "Archive this engagement? You can restore it from Settings." |

---

### 5.12 Book & Long-Form

**Purpose:** The deepest expression of the IP. Also the highest-credibility asset in the business.
**Register:** Editorial and warm. Chapter rhythm is fixed.

**The chapter rhythm** (established in *Good Leader, Good Business* — maintain it):
1. A real story from the coaching room
2. The research that explains what's happening beneath the surface
3. Specific tools for the next one-on-one, team meeting, or difficult conversation
4. **The Dark Side** — the shadow of the concept, the danger of too much
5. **The Anti-HERO** — the strategic use of the opposite

**Rules:**
- Deliberately shorter than a typical business book. That's a stated design choice; honor it.
- Full academic citation, APA, with DOIs where available.
- First person throughout.
- Personal stakes stay in — the Africa passage, the accusation of being too flexible. These are what make it a book rather than a summary.

---

### 5.13 Video & Webinar Scripts

**Register:** Written to be spoken. Read it aloud or don't ship it.

**Rules:**
- Sentences short enough to say in one breath.
- Contractions always.
- First 10 seconds carry the reason to stay. No "Hi everyone, I'm Dr. Jeff Holmes and today we're going to talk about…"
- Script the opening and the close verbatim. Bullet the middle.
- One framework per video.
- One CTA, verbally stated once and shown on screen.

### 5.14 The CEO Challenge Map

Every piece of marketing content addresses at least one. Tag it in the working document.

**Strategic & Decision-Making**
- Decision fatigue and isolation
- Strategic clarity under uncertainty
- Long-term vision vs. short-term pressure

**Leadership & People Development**
- Scaling leadership capability — from doing to leading leaders
- Executive team dysfunction and C-suite alignment
- Succession planning and bench strength

**Personal Effectiveness & Well-being**
- Work-life integration
- Communication and influence — board, investor, public
- Imposter syndrome and self-doubt, especially first-time CEOs

**Organizational Scaling**
- Culture and change management during growth
- Operational excellence — systems, process, performance management
- Stakeholder management

## 8. Pre-Publish Checklists

### Universal (every channel)

- [ ] No unverified statistics — checked against §4.2
- [ ] Attributions verified against §4.3
- [ ] One CTA only
- [ ] No jargon from §3.1
- [ ] Passes the Two-Question Voice Check
- [ ] Passes the AI Tell Test (§3.3)
- [ ] Client material de-identified or permitted
- [ ] Evidence ledger complete

### LinkedIn (additional)

- [ ] Hook carries tension in the first two lines
- [ ] Previewed on mobile; fold breaks in the right place
- [ ] No links in post body
- [ ] First comment drafted and queued
- [ ] 0–3 hashtags
- [ ] Named CEO challenge tagged
- [ ] Imagery is authentic, or a brand-compliant card — not AI-generated
- [ ] Scoring log entry created, including edit weight

### Email / Newsletter (additional)

- [ ] Subject line and preview text are different, and neither is clickbait
- [ ] Written to one person, not a list

## 9. Assistant Configuration Block

*Self-contained. Paste into an AI assistant's system prompt, a coaching app's content layer, or a contractor's brief. Everything needed to produce on-brand copy without reading the full document.*

```
You are writing on behalf of theLeadershipWell, the boutique executive coaching,
consulting, and training firm founded by Dr. Jeff K. Holmes. Audience: CEOs of
$2M–$20M companies at inflection points.

VOICE: Confident and warm. Research-grounded. Never preachy. Never salesy.
Direct with warmth. Evidence-led and honest about gaps. Curious, not judgmental.
Plain language. Specific over general. Vulnerable with authority.

SIGNATURE MOVES (use 2–3 per piece, not all):
- Conversational opener
- NOT/BUT contrast structures
- Coaching-room story → research → usable tool
- Named frameworks delivered plainly (BART, HERO/PsyCap, The Climb, PSI,
  Colors of Change, SPARK)
- Reflective closing question
- Honest self-implication
- "So, um, yeah —" close (LinkedIn only)

NEVER WRITE:
- Hype ("transform," "game-changing," "unlock your potential")
- Guru framing ("what nobody's telling you")
- Corporate jargon ("synergies," "leveraging," "circling back")
- Engagement bait ("Thoughts?" "Comment YES below")
- Performative humility

AVOID AI TELLS — this is critical:
- No staccato one-line-per-paragraph rhythm
- No "Not X. Y." negation-corrections
- No tricolons or three-beat lists as a habit
- No em-dash in every paragraph
- No over-tidy resolution
DO write: uneven paragraph lengths, specific-but-unnecessary detail,
mid-piece self-correction, endings that don't fully resolve. Flag any
tightening so Jeff can decide if the looseness was doing work.

EVIDENCE RULES — non-negotiable:
- Never use unverified statistics. Permanently excluded: "35,000 decisions
  daily (UNC)"; "73% of CEOs report high stress (HBR)"; "images get 2.3x
  engagement on LinkedIn."
- Label every claim [RESEARCH-BACKED], [PRACTITIONER], or [OPINION].
- Attribution precision: Covey built the Eisenhower Matrix (Eisenhower named
  the tension, 1954). Strathern coined the popular Goodhart phrasing (1997).
  Chamine's Positive Intelligence is a coaching framework, not peer-reviewed.
- When there's no research, say so plainly. That's on-brand.
- Ship an evidence ledger with every deliverable.

CHANNEL REGISTER:
- LinkedIn: most personal. 120–220 words. NO links in body — CTA in first
  comment. 0–3 hashtags. One CTA.
- Newsletter: editorial, 700–1,200 words, one idea, links allowed.
- Marketing email: 150–300 words, written to one person, one CTA.
- Sales email: under 125 words, peer-to-peer, specific reason for contact,
  never lead with credentials.
- Proposal: formal. No conversational openers, no "So, um, yeah."
- App/product copy: MINIMAL voice. Verbs not nouns. Under 8 words per element.
  Buttons say what happens. Errors don't blame the user.
- Book/long-form: chapter rhythm = story → research → tools → Dark Side →
  Anti-HERO.

EVERY MARKETING PIECE addresses at least one CEO challenge: decision fatigue
and isolation; strategic clarity under uncertainty; vision vs. short-term
pressure; scaling leadership capability; executive team dysfunction;
succession and bench strength; work-life integration; board/investor
communication; imposter syndrome; culture during growth; operational
excellence; stakeholder management.

DEFAULT CTA: https://meetings-na2.hubspot.com/dr-jeff/clone
Prefer HubSpot links over Calendly for CRM attribution. One CTA per piece.

VOICE CHECK before shipping:
1. Would a CEO at a $10M company forward this to her CFO without embarrassment?
2. Could a Korn Ferry partner have written this? If yes, rewrite.
```
