## What Strength Builders are (the guide's own words)

In one of their landmark studies, Zenger Folkman discovered that leaders who were highly effective in a particular competency were also highly skilled at several companion behaviors. Additionally, leaders who were highly ineffective in that competency were also highly ineffective at the same companion behaviors. Researchers do not know for certain why certain Differentiating Competencies and their companion behaviors are statistically linked. They just are!

Using these findings, Zenger Folkman concluded that perhaps the most effective way to build on a strength is to not build on it at all, but instead to build around it. Athletes know the value of building around strengths through cross-training. As an example, a gold medalist swimmer will swim practically every day, but to build endurance and lung capacity, their training often includes running, lifting weights, yoga, Pilates, and any number of other activities. These help them excel in their core competency: swimming.

Each of the 19 Differentiating Competencies has a handful of companion behaviors that are statistically linked. We have labeled these companion behaviors Strength Builders. Strength Builders represent a development path for leaders to elevate a leadership competency from good to extraordinary.

**To use the guide:**

1. Locate the Differentiating Competency you have selected as your Development Target.
2. Review the competency’s Strength Builders. – Each Strength Builder has a brief rationale statement for why the Strength Builder may be linked to the competency. – Each Strength Builder has three Development Ideas. These development ideas have been shown by empirical research to have the most substantial impact on the Strength Builder/ Differentiating Competency.
3. Select a Strength Builder that will work for your circumstances and for which you have some interest and passion.
4. Adapt the Development Ideas to fit your unique situation and create a Development Goal.
5. Create a Development Plan that outlines the specific steps you will take to improve. Be as specific and precise as possible about your actions, who you will involve, and timing.

## The 19 competencies

### Character

#### Displays High Integrity and Honesty

*Exhibits high integrity and honesty by modeling core values, matching behaviors and actions to words, and consistently delivering on commitments.*

Strength Builders: **Drive for Results** · **Build Trust** · **Be Assertive** · **Build Relationships** · **Ask for and Act on Feedback**

**Drive for Results.** People with integrity do what they say they will do, deliver the things they promise, and come through on their commitments. Having the ability to achieve results gives people with good intentions the opportunity to display their integrity and honesty.

- At the beginning of a project or task, take the time to be clear on the outcome desired and the time frame for achieving it.
- Communicate progress to stakeholders, especially when mistakes or delays impede the timely delivery of a desired outcome.
- Act with urgency. Think about ways you can demonstrate your ability to quickly adapt to changing circumstances.

**Build Trust.** Trust arises from the belief that someone is honest and ethical. The building blocks of trust include building positive relationships, exercising good judgment, and demonstrating consistency.

- Be proactive at reaching out to establish relationships throughout the organization.
- Be as transparent as possible about your decision-making processes. Think about how you can involve others in your decision-making processes, sharing important facts and information where possible.
- Identify areas where consistency is critical, for instance, in setting realistic goals and timeframes, adhering to high standards, and continuously asking how things can be improved.

**Be Assertive.** Maintaining integrity often requires asking hard questions or raising tough issues. A person who is assertive speaks up when they see things being done that are ethically questionable, or even just the “easy way out”. The path of high integrity and honesty is sometimes not the path of least resistance.

- Take on challenges. Think about how you might regularly address conflicts that you have with others quickly and effectively.
- Assume best intentions and call people to their “better selves”. Consider a goal that focuses on routinely expressing your confidence in your colleagues and teammates.
- Initiate discussions when a change is needed. Think about a goal that focuses on identifying and changing systems or processes that are not working for the team.

**Build Relationships.** Showing care and concern for others is a selfless act. Be truthful and authentic in your relationships with others.

- Stay connected. Consider learning something new about each member of your team: i.e., personal issues that affect them, important events that have meaning for them, etc.
- Build trust. Think about setting a goal to improve the skill of intentional listening and practice that skill with your teammates.
- Be inclusive. Consider how you might consistently involve others with unique or diverse perspectives in your work.
- Support others’ development. Consider setting a goal to learn about the career aspirations of your teammates and colleagues.
- Be open about yourself. Think of ways to share your own thinking, lessons learned, and new ideas.

**Ask for and Act on Feedback.** Those who seek feedback from others are thought to be unafraid of the truth, and therefore, they are also perceived to be honest and authentic.

- Make it a regular practice to ask for feedback from a variety of coworkers.
- Support an atmosphere of continual improvement. Make it a regular practice to ask for feedback on how systems and processes could be improved to make the organization better.
- Share your own development goals. Consider sharing your personal change goals with your teammates and involving them in helping you be accountable.

Linear development suggestions:

- Pass on information to others only when you believe it is accurate and complete.
- Speak truthfully at all times.
- Do not shade the truth to make things appear better than they are.
- Stand up for what you believe, even if it is not the popular position.
- Make commitments only when you are reasonably certain you can and will keep them.

### Personal Capability

#### Technical and Professional Acumen

*Demonstrates and shares exceptional expertise and job knowledge and is trusted as a significant contributor to team results.*

Strength Builders: **Solve Problems** · **Build Relationships** · **Communicate Powerfully** · **Develop Broad Perspective** · **Take Initiative** · **Act in the Team’s Best Interest**

**Solve Problems.** Solving problems is a valuable way to leverage your technical competence. Practicing problem solving may be more productive than spending time acquiring more knowledge.

- Take on challenges. Think about how you can exercise your technical and professional expertise to solve a challenging team problem.
- Focus on the long term. Consider ways to focus your problem-solving skills on a long-term organizational goal.
- Be decisive. Consider how to demonstrate your ability to make quick, effective decisions, specifically when it comes to your functional area of expertise.

**Build Relationships.** Only when a person develops strong interpersonal relationships is the full extent of their technical and professional skills seen by others. Often, positive relationships lead to the acquisition of new knowledge and information.

- Value differences. Consider a goal that will help you engage with those who think differently from you. You can discuss things such as new technologies, theories, work processes, policies, and procedures.
- Demonstrate consistency. Think of ways to isolate one aspect of your job in order to showcase your ability to consistently meet quality and time expectations.
- Support the development of others. Think of how to mentor those in the organization who are interested in your specific area of expertise.

**Communicate Powerfully.** One might have deep expertise, but if they are unable to communicate their knowledge to others, that expertise will likely go unacknowledged. Having the ability to clearly convey their expertise sets apart highly effective leaders from average leaders.

- Motivate others. Consider ways to share with others your excitement about the work you are accomplishing in your functional area.
- Focus on long-term priorities. Think of a way you might regularly communicate the changes you make in your functional area and how those changes benefit the team.
- Tailor to the individual. Think about how others might help you position key communications prior to delivering the communication.
- Build trust. Consider setting a goal whereby you routinely share your thoughts, observations, and knowledge with others.

**Develop Broad Perspective.** Those with a narrow perspective may have difficulty making connections outside of their specific area of expertise. In these cases, their knowledge and expertise might be perceived as limited and narrow. A broad perspective magnifies the number and size of problems that technical expertise can help solve.

- Embrace change. Think of a goal whereby you can build your expertise by researching how others in your area of expertise are leveraging new technology to do their jobs more effectively.
- Study other organizations. Think of how to learn about the challenges faced by other organizations in your industry and how they are meeting those challenges.
- View performance through a strategic lens. Consider a way to help team members make clear connections between the team’s performance and the organization’s strategic goals.

**Take Initiative.** The combination of expertise and initiative supplements knowledge with action. Seeking opportunities to apply your knowledge, especially outside of your work area, is a way for others to see your expertise in action.

- Focus on results. Consider the goal of learning a new skill that would help you improve your contribution to the team’s performance.
- Demonstrate integrity. Consider how to become more transparent in how you perform your functional responsibilities by sharing your processes and mindset with others.
- Anticipate problems. Consider ideas where you can proactively monitor your environment for potential problems, and address those problems before they negatively impact you, the team, or the organization.

**Act in the Team’s Best Interest.** Those who consistently put the team’s best interests first are perceived to be more trustworthy. This trust is often extended to include their technical and professional expertise. Others trust that their decisions are based on expertise, facts, and data—not biases or ulterior motives.

- Support others. Consider sharing your expertise with others in a way that will help them be more successful in their functional roles.
- Demonstrate consistency. Consider actions that will help others see your ability to consistently leverage your knowledge and expertise in support of team goals and objectives.
- Represent the team positively. Think about how you might regularly and consistently share positive information about your team members with those in other areas of the business.

Linear development suggestions:

- Read current literature relating to your field.
- Attend professional conferences.
- Enroll in university courses on subjects relating to your field.
- Listen to webinars or podcasts relating to your field.
- Participate in trade association meetings.
- Become active in a professional organization.
- Mentor a colleague interested in an area within your field of expertise.

#### Solves Problems and Analyzes Issues

*Quickly grasps the complexities of problems and uses sound analysis to make decisions, foresee obstacles, and recognize opportunities for the organization.*

Strength Builders: **Take Initiative** · **Exhibit Deep Expertise** · **Develop Strategic Perspective** · **Make Effective Decisions** · **Communicate Powerfully**

**Take Initiative.** Those who take initiative are more likely to engage in problem-solving activities. They don’t just accept that a problem must be endured. They take action to identify the source of a problem, find good solutions and take the initiative to implement the solution.

- Make a to-do list of problems that need to be resolved. To-do lists remind us of activities that often are not top priorities but nagging problems that impact productivity and effectiveness. Take on at least one of these problems every week.
- Volunteer to serve on a task force or project team that is working on process improvement.
- Anticipate potential problems. Take 15 minutes by yourself or with a group to brainstorm what could go wrong. People are fairly good at predicting potential problems, but they rarely ask themselves that question. Identify a few high potential problems and resolve them.

**Exhibit Deep Expertise.** Technical expertise is knowledge and experience; problem solving is the application of that knowledge and experience. Effective problem solvers may find they need additional knowledge to find solutions, so it is often important to engage with others in addition to relying on your own expertise.

- Expand your horizons. Look outside your organization for new technologies or professional innovations that would help solve persistent issues and problems.
- Involve others. Some leaders feel that they need to provide all the answers but end up making bad decisions. Think about who else has knowledge or experience and ask for their input.
- Identify areas where your particular expertise and experience might be of use to solve problems, even if it is not directly part of your role. Volunteer to collaborate with others to help out on project teams.

**Develop Strategic Perspective.** Having a clear view of where the organization is going will help leaders find solutions to problems that will work now and in the future.

- Keep current and conversant on news and events that affect the organization and talk to others, both inside and outside the company, about important external developments.
- Be the informant. Inform your organization and direct reports about things happening with customers, competitors, and suppliers around the world. Share your perspective on how the organization might respond to the external environment.
- Anticipate strategic problems. Look forward and consider how current practices might be problematic in the future. Many industries have been disrupted by competitors who utilized new technology. Disrupt your own organization before competitors can disrupt you.

**Make Effective Decisions.** For leaders to be seen as good problem solvers, they have to come to conclusions and make decisions in a timely manner, and then communicate them clearly. When leaders make decisions promptly, without excessive anguish, then others will assume they are good problem solvers.

- Set deadlines for decisions. Always set a deadline for a decision to be made to prevent key issues from dragging on over time.
- Maximize buy-in. Where possible, involve your direct reports and other colleagues on decisions impacting the team.
- Be willing to quickly change bad decisions. Too often leaders are slow to change decisions that are not working. When a decision is not working acknowledge the mistake and make a better decision.

**Communicate Powerfully.** When a person finds a new way to approach work, people often need to be convinced. Effective problem solvers recognize the need to communicate with others, share information, and keep other people informed

- Be proactive at sharing what you discover as you gather data and information.
- Consider the problem from the other person’s perspective. When you are communicating about an important problem, ask yourself, “What would I want to know?” and “What would be my biggest concerns?” “What value would there be for me in solving this problem?” Then, be sure to speak to those issues.
- Organize for clarity. Explain the problem, issue, or complication; discuss alternative courses of action; and then conclude with your recommendation and the reasons for it.
- Stay on message. Most communications need to be repeated. Do not be afraid to reinforce important messages multiple times.
- Repeat the “Why.” Many communications focus on the “What:” what we need to do differently, what the new process is, what behavior is inappropriate. Be sure to reinforce why we are making the change, why we are creating a new process, or why behaviors need to change.

Linear development suggestions:

- Change your mindset—see problems as opportunities, not distractions.
- Read current literature relating to problem solving.
- Enroll in professional courses on problem-solving techniques and approaches.
- Listen to webinars or podcasts on problem-solving techniques and approaches.
- Look for more problems to solve or help solve. Practice makes perfect.

#### Innovates

*Consistently generates, encourages, and supports creative and resourceful solutions to improve the organization.*

Strength Builders: **Inspire Others** · **Champion Change** · **Show Courage** · **Communicate Powerfully** · **Develop Strategic Perspective**

**Inspire Others.** Innovating is difficult and helping people overcome their fears takes a very special skill. Successful innovators are able to motivate others to accept and implement innovative advancements. In addition, skilled innovators are often seen as inspirational.

- Set stretch goals. Think of ways to involve employees from other parts of the organization who have a positive innovative track record, to help you and your team set innovative stretch goals
- Communicate a clear vision. Consider how to recognize colleagues who are working on new, innovative solutions for the organization; help others make the connection of how those innovations will positively impact the organization’s long-term goals.
- Boost collaboration. Learn about innovations that motivate or inspire you personally and share that information with your team.
- Encourage team members to offer new ideas and recognize suggestions for improvement.

**Champion Change.** Those who embrace change will naturally encourage innovation. In addition, a change effort often leads to innovative solutions. These two behaviors are tightly linked.

- Act quickly. Consider ways you can demonstrate your ability to make quick, effective decisions in implementing necessary innovations.
- Share external perspective. Learn about how other organizations are promoting and supporting an innovative culture; share your learnings with other stakeholders.
- Quickly recognize the need for change. Think about how to generate a sense of urgency among key stakeholders for a needed change in your area.

**Show Courage.** Those who are unwilling to step forward and take risks will not be successful innovators. Innovation, by its very nature, requires courage and risk.

- Promote a new change. Consider sharing an innovative change with your team or other key stakeholders that would improve your performance or the team’s performance.
- Challenge status quo. Consider ways your team can safely challenge current processes to find innovative ways to increase the team’s ability to deliver expected results.
- Be willing to engage with customers to uncover where their needs can be met by better processes or other improvements.

**Communicate Powerfully.** Meaningful innovations happen when people share ideas with each other and collaborate to bring those ideas to fruition. Innovation is turning words into a shared achievement.

- Motivate others. Consider ways to share your excitement about the innovation in your functional area with others.
- Focus on long-term priorities. Think of a way you might regularly communicate the innovative things you are doing within your functional area and how those innovations benefit the team.
- Tailor to the individual. Think about how to establish a pattern of including those with diverse perspectives in discussions focused on improving the team’s performance through innovation.
- Build trust. Consider how you might routinely recognize colleagues who have endeavored to implement innovative change for the organization, even if those innovations were not successful.

**Develop Strategic Perspective.** People with little strategic perspective have difficulty seeing the value of innovation pursuits or linking innovative pursuits to the success of the organization. Innovation that is not clearly integrated with strategy is often not valued by the organization.

- Communicate clearly and often. Consider how to become more effectively conversant about new technologies that might improve the performance of your team or organization.
- Connect with customers. Consider a goal whereby you learn about the issues affecting your customers, and bring forward innovative opportunities to improve the customer experience.
- Motivate others. Regularly research specific innovative questions or topics of interest and involve others who share those same interests.

Linear development suggestions:

- Have a change of mindset—deliberately suspend the part of you that says, “It will not work.”
- Read current literature relating to innovative success stories.
- Enroll in professional courses or certification programs focused on innovation.
- Listen to webinars or podcasts on innovative techniques and approaches.

#### Learning Agility

*Takes ownership for understanding oneself by learning from personal successes and failures, applying self-knowledge to professional situations, seeking and accepting feedback from others, and proactively seeking learning opportunities.*

Strength Builders: **Listen and Learn** · **Stretch Yourself** · **Project Optimism** · **Demonstrate Self-Honesty**

**Listen and Learn.** Those who are agile learners are also skilled listeners. It is through listening that one can learn a lot about themself.

- Avoid being defensive. Think about how to leverage a coach or mentor to help you with a personal change that you would like to make.
- Build trust. Create a goal that helps you listen to others, focusing on the substance of the message and the speaker’s non-verbal cues.
- Build positive relationships. Try to become more mindful of how often you speak during a conversation; consider setting a goal to speak only 25% during the conversations you have with colleagues.

**Stretch Yourself.** Highly effective agile learners are willing to take on situations outside of their comfort zone. Agile learners thrive on the learning opportunities presented by steep learning curves and the potential for failure.

- Focus on results. Consider setting a series of ambitious goals for yourself that support improving overall team deliverables.
- Motivate others. Consider ways to learn something new (in or out of the business) that is of interest to you; try to involve others on your team.
- Address problems. Consider a goal whereby you confront a personal fear or anxiety; i.e., talking in front of others, meeting new people, etc.

**Project Optimism.** Agile learners are generally optimistic. They are skilled at seeing the positive side of failures and they move on from negative situations quickly.

- Motivate others. Regularly express your optimism about the organization’s work or the specific work that you do for the organization.
- Respect others. Think about how you might verbally express your personal confidence in your teammates and other colleagues.

**Demonstrate Self-Honesty.** Agile learners are more prone to face facts about themselves. Conversely, those who demonstrate self-honesty are more agile learners.

- Apply learnings to new challenges. Think about how to establish a weekly pattern of personal reflection, reviewing the week’s successes and missteps and how to apply those learnings in the coming week.
- Ask for and act on feedback. Consider setting a goal that will help you establish a pattern of regularly asking others for feedback about your performance.
- Maintain objectivity. Think of ways to leverage your strengths and acknowledge your limitations. For example, when taking on a new task or project, ask yourself, “Can I solve this on my own or do I need help from others?”

Linear development suggestions:

- Have a change of mindset. View successes and mistakes as learning opportunities that can be applied in the future.
- Identify confidants who will challenge your perspective and help you see your areas of strength as well as areas where you can develop.
- Place yourself in situations where you need to learn new skills, where your current skills are stretched to their maximum, or where you have the opportunity to make mistakes.
- Establish a routine of reflecting on your professional performance. Identify patterns that will be helpful to you in the future.
- Listen to webinars or podcasts on topics relating to learning agility such as resilience, adaptive learning, self-awareness, etc.

### Focus on Results

#### Drives for Results

*Drives for results by holding oneself and others accountable, persisting in the face of obstacles, and doing everything possible to achieve goals.*

Strength Builders: **Take Initiative** · **Act Quickly** · **Build Trust** · **Establish Stretch Goals** · **Anticipate Problems** · **Develop Strategic Perspective**

**Take Initiative.** It is easy to get complacent in our work. Those who are highly effective at delivering results are those who regularly take the initiative to do whatever it takes to deliver quality work on time.

- Be proactive. Identifying better ways to get things done through streamlined processes, faster delivery times, improved quality, etc.
- Delight customers. Regularly consider what it would take to delight a customer (internal or external).
- Be an early adopter. Be quick to adopt new technology.
- Take on an unmet opportunity that would add value to personal or team deliverables; involve your team members in your plans.

**Act Quickly.** Speed provides individuals and organizations with a significant competitive advantage. Speed has a way of cutting through red tape and bureaucracy, providing a laser focus on the end goal.

- Focus on the long term. Consider ideas that would help you or your team members to maintain a focus on the end goals, thereby increasing your ability to act more quickly in delivering results.
- Be decisive. Consider ways you can demonstrate your ability to make quick, effective decisions, specifically when it comes to delivering expected results.
- Challenge the status quo. Consider ideas whereby you and your team can safely challenge current processes to find ways to provide higher quality deliverables in a faster timeframe.

**Build Trust.** Those who consistently deliver results are perceived as being honest and trustworthy. Conversely, those who are honest are thought to keep their commitments, including in delivering agreed-upon results.

- Build positive relationships. Consider a goal that focuses on sharing knowledge or information with a cross-functional team that directly impacts the performance of that team.
- Exercise good judgment. Think about a way to demonstrate your ability to exercise good judgment, specifically when it comes to delivering expected results.
- Demonstrate consistency. Consider how to increase your ability to consistently deliver expected results: regularly monitoring progress, identifying road blocks, etc.

**Establish Stretch Goals.** Those who are highly effective at driving results recognize that setting ambitious goals with their team will boost performance significantly. Teams can become complacent— performing at the same level. A stretch goal can inspire the team to reach performance levels they never thought possible.

- Motivate others. Consider ways you can involve your team members in setting game-changing goals for the team to pursue.
- Anticipate and address problems. Consider ways you can anticipate problems and establish goals for addressing those problems before they negatively impact your performance or the team’s performance.
- Focus on the long term. Consider actions that will help you highlight your ability to leverage stretch goals in the pursuit of achieving the long-term goals of the team or the larger organization.

**Anticipate Problems.** The ability to anticipate problems is highly correlated with producing results. By thinking ahead, “How will our customers react if we change this process? How will we respond?” leaders are able to prepare and maintain their ability to deliver results.

- Look for trends. Consider ways you can identify patterns in your work or the team’s work which signal that there might be problems ahead.
- Make quick decisions. Consider how you can demonstrate your ability to make quick, effective decisions, specifically when it comes to problems impacting the team’s performance.
- Initiate change early. Think of specific things you can do that will help others see your ability to quickly change course when things aren’t working.

**Develop Strategic Perspective.** Driving results without a clear vision of the end goal is like trying to assemble a jigsaw puzzle blindfolded. Highly effective, results-driven leaders have a clear picture of what the outcome will look like.

- Communicate goals. Consider ways you can routinely clarify and reinforce the team’s objectives for yourself and for the team.
- Foster innovation. Consider how to introduce innovative changes to help improve your performance or the team’s performance.
- Focus on the customer. Think of specific actions you can take to help others see your ability to connect the team’s performance goals to changing customer needs and expectations.

Linear development suggestions:

- Involve team members in identifying Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to help you keep your work on course.
- Create visuals to keep timelines in view for yourself and team members.
- Expand your network to include additional or alternative resources to help you achieve results.
- Ensure that those you lead have clear plans for how they will accomplish their goals. Review those plans with them; provide input and support.
- Follow up with team members regularly to ensure deadlines and deliverables are being achieved.
- Identify a team member or team project that has lost momentum. Take steps to help get things back on track.
- Listen to webinars or podcasts about time management, achieving results, etc.

#### Establishes Stretch Goals

*Focuses self and others on high priorities by seeing beyond standard goals, setting clear stretch goals, and modeling standards of excellence.*

Strength Builders: **Drive for Results** · **Inspire Others** · **Solve Problems** · **Develop Strategic Perspective**

**Drive for Results.** True stretch goals are not easy to achieve. Achieving stretch goals creates a tremendous high, but failing to come close can create great discouragement. One must be totally dedicated to accomplishing the goal and do everything possible to achieve it.

- Take on challenges. Consider setting a series of development goals for yourself or your team that will support improving team deliverables.
- Build trust. Consider a goal that is focused on building up others through recognition, specifically when they achieve challenging or highly ambitious goals.
- Act quickly. Consider ways you can demonstrate your ability to make quick, effective decisions, specifically when it comes to achieving challenging goals or delivering results.

**Inspire Others.** For teams to embrace stretch goals, leaders need to be inspirational. Without someone inspirational leading the way, stretch goals look like just more work. In that situation, team members will resist and eventually negotiate lower standards and less aggressive goals. Leaders who can inspire and motivate others to higher levels of effort and production will have teams that reach stretch goals.

- Set stretch goals. Think about establishing a personal stretch goal, sharing your goal with team members, and asking for their support in helping you accomplish your goal.
- Communicate a clear vision. Consider how best to communicate the organization’s strategic goals in a way that encourages individual team members to set stretch goals for their functional areas.
- Boost collaboration. Consider a way you might regularly recognize team members who are working towards or who have achieved a difficult goal.

**Solve Problems.** Those who are highly effective at establishing and achieving stretch goals are also highly effective problem solvers. Without problem solving, a stretch goal can only be accomplished by working harder, faster, or longer. Accomplishing stretch goals often means solving problems with processes, relationships, partnerships, and infrastructure.

- Take on challenges. Consider a goal whereby you analyze a team project that is not progressing as expected and create a plan to move the project forward.
- Focus on the long term. Examine ways to learn how other businesses in your field have leveraged stretch goals to solve problems in the industry and apply your findings to your work.
- Be decisive. Consider how to demonstrate your ability to select and establish challenging goals that will make a difference for the team.

**Develop Strategic Perspective.** Those who are highly effective at setting and achieving stretch goals have a clear vision of the organization’s goals. Without a strategic perspective, one can set goals that do not accomplish the organization’s purposes.

- Communicate clearly and often. Consider how to leverage a new technology or process to improve the team’s performance and support the organization’s strategic goals.
- Demonstrate innovation. Consider a goal whereby you engage your team in regular brainstorming activities aimed at identifying innovative stretch goals that would positively impact the team’s performance.
- Connect with customers. Consider a goal whereby you keep current on the issues affecting your customers, specifically bringing forward new ideas for challenging team goals that would improve the customer experience.

Linear development suggestions:

- Have a change of mindset—focus on why you can achieve lofty goals rather than on why they cannot be achieved.
- Enroll in professional courses or certification programs about goal setting and achieving.
- Listen to webinars or podcasts relating to establishing stretch goals.
- Generate agreement among group members about the team’s stretch goals.
- Set measurable milestones that can be celebrated when achieved.
- Foster confidence in others that goals will be achieved, by regularly expressing your optimism.
- Promote a spirit of continuous improvement by sharing your goals with others.
- Maintain high standards of personal performance.

- Volunteer to develop protocol for keeping your virtual team connected and informed.
- Involve all team members when establishing plans and time lines for accomplishing team goals.
- Pass on credit for your accomplishments to the whole team.
- Initiate team activities that will build team relationships. Encourage these activities cross- functionally.
- Refer questions to the appropriate team member. Team members who feel respected are more collaborative.
- Help resolve team conflict. If it goes too long, it will negatively impact collaboration and the work environment.
- Volunteer for cross-functional assignments and encourage others from your work group to get involved with you.
- Promote cross-functional projects. Encourage those you lead to get involved in projects outside of their immediate work areas.

#### Values Diversity

*Builds an inclusive climate by encouraging open discussions of differences, demonstrating an appreciation for others regardless of differences, and leveraging diverse perspectives to accomplish objectives.*

Strength Builders: **Collaborate and Promote Teamwork** · **Build Relationships** · **Ask for and Act on Feedback** · **Develop Others** · **Inspire Others**

**Collaborate and Promote Teamwork.** Those who effectively practice inclusion tend to be collaborative. The reason for this might be because they find value in the perspectives and views of those who think differently than them. People who do not value diversity tend to build relationships with people that think, act, and look like them, which reduces their effectiveness.

- Build diverse relationships. Set a goal that involves working on collaborative projects with colleague/s whose views regularly differ from yours.
- Display energy and excitement. Regularly share an optimistic statement or point of view about your team with others.
- Communicate clearly and often. Develop a habit of gathering input from diverse individuals on how best to compose key communications.

**Build Relationships.** By practicing inclusion, all team members feel valued and appreciated for their differences and unique perspectives. Such feelings are a solid foundation for strong working relationships. It is difficult for one to build strong working relationships, if they routinely dismiss or disregard groups of people based on a personal bias.

- Involve others with different perspectives. Develop a habit of gathering alternative perspectives.
- Demonstrate consistency. Consider how you might consistently demonstrate an open and unbiased perspective when working with others.
- Defuse conflict. Reflect on how you might routinely approach and ultimately minimize conflict in your working relationships and between team members.

**Ask for and Act on Feedback.** One who is open to feedback has a better understanding of how their behavior impacts those around them, making them highly effective team members. In addition, one who values diversity finds value in the perceptions of others, which is the hallmark behavior for soliciting feedback.

- Build trusting relationships. Think about ways you can show your steadfast support and respect for those whose life experiences and perspectives are different from yours.
- Support an atmosphere of continual improvement. Establish a pattern of regularly asking others for feedback on aspects of your performance.
- Share your development goals. Set a goal that encourages you to share your personal change goals with your teammates—specifically those who have perspectives that differ from yours— and involve them in helping you be accountable.

**Develop Others.** Developing others is a clear expression of concern and caring. One would not likely invest the time, effort, and money in developing another if they did not care for the person. Practicing inclusion is also an expression of care and concern. These two behaviors are likely linked by this common root.

- Motivate others. Learn about the career aspirations of your colleagues—specifically those whose life experiences are different from yours—and support and motivate them to achieve their goals.
- Demonstrate care and concern. Set a goal in which you mentor a colleague that has views and perspectives that differ from your own.
- Communicate clearly and often. Consider how you might routinely share development ideas or opportunities with those you lead.

**Inspire Others.** Those who recognize the value of all human-kind are inspirational. In addition, to be highly effective at motivating performance, people must be able to understand and relate to others.

- Set stretch goals. Involve those who have different backgrounds than you in helping you set a functional or team stretch goal.
- Communicate clearly and often. Learn what commonalities you share with your teammates and leverage that knowledge to improve your communication with them.
- Boost collaboration. Recognize and express your personal confidence in colleagues outside of your team and with whom you rarely interact.

Linear development suggestions:

- Commit to the diversity goals and vision of your organization.
- Question policies that may cause some employees to feel devalued or disrespected.
- Take measures to ensure that team activities do not favor some and possibly exclude others.
- Actively challenge systems and processes that put specific groups at a disadvantage for selection, development, or promotion.
- Volunteer or serve on committees that organize diversity-related events and activities.
- Openly recognize colleagues who demonstrate inclusive behaviors.
- Become familiar with diversity-related terms and, if you err, apologize and ask for help.
- Do not permit language or the telling of jokes that are offensive to others.
- Be tolerant of others who do not yet demonstrate behaviors of inclusiveness; look for opportunities to teach and model the appropriate behaviors.

### Leading Change

#### Develops Strategic Perspective

*Has a long-term perspective of the organization’s goals by understanding key business drivers, navigating ambiguity and complexity, and helping others understand the organization’s vision and objectives.*

Strength Builders: **Communicate Powerfully** · **Foster Innovation** · **Focus on the Customer** · **Inspire Others** · **Establish Stretch Goals**

**Communicate Powerfully.** If one is unable to communicate a strategy in a way that others can understand it and embrace it, the strategy means nothing. Worse, it will be impossible for a strategy to be successfully implemented. Misunderstanding or lack of information is often at the root of strategic missteps.

- Motivate others. Consider ways to build excitement and energy around the accomplishments of the team and how they connect to the success of the larger organization.
- Tailor to the individual. Think about how to involve others in helping you position key strategic communications to specific groups.
- Build trust. Think about ways you can transparently demonstrate how your work and decisions contribute to the long-term success of the team and the organization.

**Foster Innovation.** Strategies that only mimic competitors have the impossible task of creating a competitive advantage. For strategies to be distinctive, they need to be innovative and challenge the status quo. Finding unique or distinctive approaches can significantly improve a strategy’s effectiveness.

- Motivate others. Think of how you might routinely recognize the accomplishments of colleagues who are highly innovative in their approach to their work.
- Initiate change. Consider a goal in which you identify a current process that may be preventing your team or the organization from reaching it’s goals; research solution options and share your findings with stakeholders.
- Be courageous. Consider setting a goal that prompts you to routinely (and respectfully) ask questions that challenge the status quo.
- Communicate clearly and often. Consider a goal through which you regularly communicate your support of innovations taking place that have a direct connection to the organization’s strategic goals.

**Focus on the Customer.** Your customers are the most important part of your business. Strategic planning should begin with an understanding of what the customer wants and needs. Furthermore, by focusing on customers, people learn important information that can inform strategic decisions.

- Initiate change. Consider a goal whereby you keep current on the issues affecting your customers, specifically bringing forward change opportunities that would improve the customer experience.
- Communicate clearly and often. Consider setting a goal whereby you routinely share your thoughts and observations about how the customer experience is key to the long-term success of the organization.

**Inspire Others.** Some believe that a strong, compelling strategy will, by itself, encourage people in the organization to work and act accordingly. This is rarely true. Inspirational people are able to get team members fully committed and enthusiastic about the organization’s strategic goals.

- Set stretch goals. Think of a way to involve your teammates in setting a challenging stretch goal that would directly impact the organization’s strategic goals.
- Communicate a clear vision. Consider a goal whereby you learn how to integrate memorable stories, examples, or illustrations that will help you explain the organization’s vision to those you lead.
- Boost collaboration. Consider how to identify a new skill or technology that would support the organization’s strategic objectives and arrange for a group-learning experience.

**Establish Stretch Goals.** A strategic initiative nearly always involves goals that require everyone in the organization to stretch in some way. It might mean finding new ways to do something faster or better, develop a new product, etc. One who can establish clear stretch goals and gain the commitment of others to accomplish those goals will be highly effective at reaching a strategic vision.

- Focus on results. Consider setting a series of ambitious goals for yourself or your team that support improving the team’s contribution to the organization.
- Motivate others. Consider ways you can involve your team members in setting team goals that support the organization’s strategic goals.
- Anticipate and address problems. Consider ideas where you can help identify potential problems before those problems negatively impact your performance or the team’s performance.

Linear development suggestions:

- Listen to webinars or podcasts on highly effective strategic leaders. Listen for common themes and behaviors and implement them where appropriate.
- Read books and articles and attend seminars and webinars on strategic planning and thinking. – The writings of Ram Charan, C.K. Prahalad, Gary Hamel, Michael Porter, and Todd Zenger are good places to start. One specific article that might be of interest is ‘Value Lab: A tool for Entrepreneurial Strategy” by Teppo Felin (Utah State University & Oxford University), Alfonso Gambardella (Bocconi University), and Todd Zenger (University of Utah).
- Read appropriate publications and talk to others, both inside and outside of the company, to stay up-to-date on news and events that affect the business.
- Join professional organizations that will build your knowledge and allow you to meet your counterparts from other organizations.
- Define the characteristics that set your organization apart from others in the industry.
- Learn about the issues and concerns of the organization’s senior leaders so you can talk about those issues when the opportunity presents itself.
- Read articles by the organization’s senior executives.
- Attend relevant trade shows to identify new opportunities and trends in the industry and to find out what your competitors are doing.

#### Champions Change

*Demonstrates the passion and courage to initiate, support, and drive change while helping others embrace and implement change.*

Strength Builders: **Foster Innovation** · **Act Quickly** · **Develop Strategic Perspective** · **Develop External Perspective** · **Inspire Others**

**Foster Innovation.** When introducing change, it is rarely clear how the organization will get from the current state to the future state. Frequently, new and innovative approaches are needed for the change to be successful. The ability to innovate and implement change are two skills that are tightly linked.

- Motivate others. Think of how you might routinely recognize the accomplishments of colleagues who are highly change-oriented in their work.
- Be courageous. Consider setting a goal that prompts you to routinely (and respectfully) ask questions that challenge the status quo.
- Communicate clearly and often. Consider a goal through which you regularly communicate your support of changes happening in other parts of the organization.

**Act Quickly.** Removing a bandage slowly can be much more painful than ripping it off quickly. Once planned and organized, a change initiative should be executed as quickly as possible. Speed provides focus, removes unnecessary barriers, and energizes those implementing the change. Those who are able to act quickly will be more successful at championing change.

- Focus on the long term. Consider ideas that would help you and your team members maintain a focus on the end goals, thereby increasing your ability to quickly initiate and implement changes when needed.
- Make quick decisions. Consider ways you can demonstrate your ability to make quick, effective decisions, specifically when it comes to implementing change within the team or your functional area.
- Challenge the status quo. Consider ideas whereby you and your team can safely challenge current processes to find ways to increase the team’s ability to provide higher quality deliverables in a faster timeframe.

**Develop Strategic Perspective.** One of the most important Strength Builders in implementing change is having a clear strategic direction. Helping others catch the vision for a change is a critical step in creating a successful change effort. With a clearly shared vision, people have a sense of purpose and are independently able to anticipate problems and respond more effectively to difficulties.

- Communicate clearly and often. Consider an idea that will help you routinely communicate your support for changes being implemented within the team or within the larger organization.
- Connect with customers. Consider a goal whereby you keep current on the issues affecting your customers, specifically bringing forward change opportunities that would improve the customer experience.
- Motivate others. Think of how you might routinely bring to your team something you find personally motivating this might be a guest speaker, video, new technology, etc. that has a change focus.

**Develop External Perspective.** At times of significant change, individuals tend to focus internally. Where is our organization having problems? Why didn’t this initiative work? Those with an external focus are able to see the bigger picture. Are we moving forward? Are the changes making a difference to our market share? How are our competitors responding to the market forces? Those with an external perspective are key to change initiatives.

- Connect with customers. Create a plan to connect directly or indirectly with customers regularly for the purpose of identifying helpful trends; share your findings with stakeholders.
- Become a student of strategy. Develop a habit to keep current and conversant on disruptive forces that might affect the organization; share the information with others, both internal and external to the organization.
- Sell your insights internally. Consider a goal that will help you share your customer and market insights with key stakeholders on a regular basis.

**Inspire Others.** People often start change efforts by communicating clear directions and expectations. This might be described as a push in the new direction. However, for a change to be successful, people also need to be excited and energized to do difficult, unfamiliar things. This could be described as a pull in the new direction. Those who can inspire others and pull them in the new direction are essential for a successful change effort.

- Set stretch goals. Think of a way to involve employees from other parts of the organization who have a positive innovative track record to help you and your team set an innovative stretch goal.
- Communicate a clear vision. Consider how to share with your team members the benefits of a specific change taking place within the team or the larger organization.
- Boost collaboration. Set a goal whereby you regularly express your support for a controversial change taking place in another part of the organization.

Linear development suggestions:

- Have a change of mindset—deliberately suspend the part of you that resists change.
- Make it a habit to identify the benefits of organizational changes. Share your insights with others.
- Listen to webinars or podcasts on highly effective change leaders. Identify common themes and behaviors and implement them where appropriate.
- Read books and articles and attend seminars on how to create and support a change culture.
- Read appropriate publications and talk to others, both inside and outside of the company, to stay up to date on news and events that affect the business.
- Spend time thinking about and researching what organizational changes could increase its profitability or market share.
- When you see opportunities for improvement, tactfully share your insights with stakeholders.
- Join professional organizations that will connect you with others who are informed about internal and external changes that affect the organization.

#### Customer and External Focus

*Connects with customers and the external environment by networking effectively and leveraging relationships to benefit the organization.*

Strength Builders: **Develop Strategic Perspective** · **Champion Change** · **Inspire Others** · **Communicate Powerfully** · **Establish Stretch Goals**

**Develop Strategic Perspective.** Those with a solely tactical perspective see little value in and often find it difficult to connect with the external world. By looking beyond the day to day work and focusing on the strategic vision, one can see the importance of understanding their customers, the external environment, and their connection to the organization’s success.

- Communicate clearly and often. Consider an idea that will help you develop a habit of expressing the importance of the customer experience to the success of the organization.
- Demonstrate innovation. Consider researching how other organizations within your industry have leveraged new technologies to increase their market share.
- Motivate others. Think of how you might routinely recognize the accomplishments of colleagues who are customer facing.

**Champion Change.** Those who have the ability to gain insights from customers and the external environment that will benefit the organization will be successful. One who uses those insights as a catalyst for organizational change will be even more successful.

- Demonstrate innovation. Think of how to introduce a new skill or technology to the team that would improve the team’s ability to deliver a quality client experience.
- Act quickly. Consider ways you can demonstrate your ability to quickly identify situations where immediate actions and initiative are needed, specifically in support of the client experience.
- Share external perspective. Consider a goal whereby you learn about changes happening within key client organizations that you could share with interested stakeholders.

**Inspire Others.** One who radiates enthusiasm and excitement for the business and emotionally connects with their customer base, suppliers, investors, and trade association members will establish strong, loyal, long-term relationships. Customers need to know that they are purchasing the best product or service from the best provider. A customer that you inspire, is a customer you will keep.

- Set stretch goals. Think of a way to involve customers or customer data in helping you to set a functional or team stretch goal.
- Communicate clearly and often. Consider learning something that is motivating or inspiring about one of your clients and share that client’s story with your team.
- Boost collaboration. Consider a way to learn the similarities you share with your customer contacts and how to leverage those similarities to improve customer interactions.

**Communicate Powerfully.** To successfully maintain connections with clients and external stakeholders requires highly effective communication skills. Delivering meaningful messages and information that connect with those outside of the organization requires great effort and skill.

- Motivate others. Consider a goal whereby you study specific problems facing your clients and regularly share your findings with others who may find the topic of interest.
- Tailor to the individual. Think about how to involve others in helping you position key strategic communications to specific customer groups (internal and external).
- Build trust. Consider ways you can practice the skill of intentional listening with clients on a regular basis.

**Establish Stretch Goals.** Those who are highly effective at setting and achieving stretch goals often have a clear picture of their customer’s wants and needs. They successfully use customer knowledge to set meaningful goals that will improve the customer experience.

- Focus on results. Consider setting a series of ambitious goals for yourself or your team that support improving the client experience.
- Motivate others. Consider ways you can involve your team members in setting game-changing goals for the team to pursue.
- Anticipate and address problems. Consider ideas where you can help identify potential problems before those problems negatively impact your performance or the team’s performance.
- Focus on the long-term. Consider actions that will help you highlight your ability to leverage stretch goals in the pursuit of achieving the long-term goals of the team or the larger organization.
