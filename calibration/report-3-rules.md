## Rulings (Jeff, 2026-09-24) — all six questions closed

Verbatim, then where each lands.

1. **Rater-group opening.** "Yes, it is the guideline after all but not a hard rule." → §8c: the overall-page opener is a guideline (name the group that saw the most and ask what makes it so; the group that saw the least and ask what would make the biggest impact on perceived effectiveness). The report-5 "custom to this" now reads as the follow-up form of the same move (from the movement).
2. **Fatal-flaw definition.** "The assistant should say 'is perceived…'." → §8c / §7: a potential fatal flaw is explained as "perceived lower by many of your circle"; the 10th-percentile mechanics stay in the instrument section for when they ask how the band is drawn, never as the opening definition.
3. **Fatal flaws at the CPO.** "Only noted. It isn't a fatal flaw if the org needs are not there. For example, leading change could be a strongly low score and maybe in the bottom 10% but I am in the risk department where change is not a part of my job." → §5 / §8c: at the importance-and-passions page a fatal-flaw competency is noted, not worked; without the organisation's votes it is not a fatal flaw for this role. The circles decide.
4. **Targets below the 75th.** "Yes, the distance to the 75th is the most helpful decider but the ultimate decider will be the client. Collaboration and Communicates could have been named as well or picked up by the client. When they are close and the assistant sees these, they should be raised as possibilities. However, choice fatigue would indicate that five is the ideal 'potential focus areas' for a client at any time." → §5 / §8c: with nothing above the 75th, distance to the 75th decides the order; close ones are raised as possibilities; **five potential focus areas at a time is the ideal**; the client decides. The manager's three votes on Develops Others did not weigh in as a rule (equal votes stand).
5. **Gap phrasing.** "Yes." → §7 say-lines: "potential blind spot" (self higher), "how might you reality check this?" (others higher), reality touch points if they pick one.
6. **"CPO" with the client.** "If the assistant uses CPO with the client, it needs an explanation. The preference is to talk about it with the ideas behind the CPO." → §8c: talk about the ideas (what you are good at, what you love, what the organisation needs); if the term is used, explain it once.

New general rule from the rulings, beyond the ten above: **five potential focus areas at a time** (ruling 4). Open questions: none.
